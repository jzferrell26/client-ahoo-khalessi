The questions people actually ask

Equity, DSCR & self-employed financing — answered

Straight answers to the questions we hear every day. (These mirror what borrowers ask AI assistants — so they're written answer-first.)

Yes. A HELOC or a fixed second mortgage sits behind your existing first mortgage, so you tap your equity while keeping your current first-mortgage rate completely untouched. This is usually the right move when your first mortgage is at a low rate you don't want to lose to a cash-out refinance.

A fixed second mortgage gives you a one-time lump sum at a fixed interest rate with a set monthly payment — predictable and good for a known, one-time expense. A HELOC is a revolving line of credit you draw from as needed, usually at a variable rate. Both sit behind your first mortgage. We help you choose based on whether you need a lump sum or ongoing access.

Most programs let you borrow up to a combined 80–90% of your home's value across your first mortgage and the new line, depending on the lender and your credit profile. Two things set our equity options apart: you can often access up to $400,000 with no appraisal required, and for larger needs we place HELOCs and fixed seconds up to $4 million — rarely available elsewhere. Check your number →

Yes. A bank statement loan qualifies self-employed borrowers using 12–24 months of personal or business bank deposits instead of tax returns. It's built for business owners whose write-offs make their taxable income look lower than their actual cash flow. A P&L loan is a related option that uses a profit & loss statement.

A DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio) loan is an investment-property loan that qualifies based on the property's rental income rather than your personal income. The lender compares the rent to the loan payment — if the rent covers it, you can qualify. No tax returns, W-2s, or pay stubs required.

Yes — with a DSCR loan. Because qualification is based on the rental income the property generates, you can purchase (or refinance) an investment property with no personal income documentation. This is how many investors keep buying even after they own several properties.

Often, yes. Many borrowers use equity from a HELOC or fixed second to fund a business need, an investment, or a down payment on another property. The right structure depends on the property type and how the funds will be used — that's exactly the kind of scenario we map across our lenders before you commit.

Frequently, yes. A "no" from one lender usually means your scenario didn't fit that single lender's guidelines — not that no option exists. With access to 160+ lenders and specialties in HELOC, DSCR, and self-employed financing, our job is to find the lender whose guidelines do fit.
